# The Wheel

The board had answered him by the time he came back, and the answer was a silence he could read.

He had expected the message that does not come. No claim contests, no jeers, no broker's flag. What he had not expected was the one private line, routed long, stripped of its header: *Have you been inside.* No question mark. Someone careful had taken the punctuation off on purpose, so it would not look like a thing he could answer with a yes or a no.

He had not been inside. He had chipped a flake off the outside in the dark and run from what it meant for a season, then spent money he did not have to come back. The *Margin* hung off the long axis again, the loop ticking on the bad grease, the question sitting where the file used to sit.

He went inside because the alternative was to keep not going inside, and a man could only carry that so long. He suited up by the numbers. He checked the loop twice and the rebreather three times because Devi was in the cabin telling him to. He did not argue. She was right and the arguing was over.

◆

He had been treating it as a fragment. That word had let him keep it small. You could hold a fragment in a glove. A fragment had a shape that ended.

The headlamp found the surface a hundred meters out and the surface did not end.

It curved. All season he had carried a picture of a broken thing—a slab, a shard, a hull section tumbling. The lamp showed him a curve that did not stop where a fragment's curve stopped. It ran out of the cone of the light and he tracked it and it kept going, the same regular swell, the corrugation running away into the dark both ways like the staves of something too big to be a thing. He read scale off a hull at a glance, thirty years of it in his body. He was looking at the inside of a curve whose center was off in the dark behind him, and the radius was kilometers.

He stopped on the tether and let himself hang. He turned the lamp slow along the corrugation and counted the staves into the dark until counting stopped meaning anything, and the cold he had felt in the cabin a season ago came back. Not the cabin's cold. The one the body let in at its own rate.

The radar had told him this and he had not believed it, because the number belonged to a moon, to a captured rock, not to a made thing, and a made thing was salvage and salvage had a size you could tow. He believed it now. The two facts sat in his chest—made, and kilometers—not reconciling, just both true, the floor gone out under them.

Ahead the corrugation broke into a darker geometry, a recess the lamp could not flatten. He went to it hand over hand, breathing the way you make a panicked man breathe, by counting. It was a way in.

◆

It had been a port once. He knew it the way you know a thing your trade has taught your hands. A frame. A sealing surface. A place where something had mated and unmated under pressure, the same problem he solved every time he cycled a lock, solved by someone else, long ago. The closure was gone, the dark inside a dark with walls.

He went in. The lamp threw its circle on the inner surface and the surface was a floor.

He knew it by the grammar of it. It ran flat under his boots for a stride, then began to rise, to curve up and away ahead and behind and to either side, the whole of it tilting into a vault he could not see the top of. His trade-trained eye and his inner ear, which had nothing to read in free fall, reported the same thing: this was the inside of a wheel, and the floor was the floor because the wheel had turned once and thrown a world's weight outward against it, and held it there. He ran the lamp up the curve and the floor went up and over, past where the beam died, and came down the other side, upside down to him, waiting for a weight that was not there.

The dread came in proper then. Not fear—fear was fast and clean, a loop that failed, a tether that parted, over quick. This was the slow one, the one that arrived at the rate the body allowed. He was standing inside a place made to be lived in, with a ground and a sky's worth of vault over it, where the weight under his feet was the weight a turning world made so the body would have a down to stand on and a curve overhead to call a sky.

Somebody had built a sky. Out here. Sixty million years cold. They had built it because they were coming out here to stay, and they had wanted to stand on something that felt like home.

He had thought he had found a thing. He had found a place. A place built to be lived in, empty a long time, was not a wreck. It was a tomb. The size of a city.

◆

He made himself work, because the procedure was the only thing between him and a man who lets a tomb take him apart. He photographed the floor with the *Margin*'s ranging laser laid across the curve, so the radius would be in the frame, so no one could call it a slab. He photographed the staves from the inside, where they were ribs, the alloy he had massed a season ago laid up exactly the way he would have laid it up. Where a section of skin had peeled he found a depth of soil behind it, a made ground over the structural floor, the way you lay a ground if you mean for things to grow. He did not let himself think the word *field*.

High up the curve he found the run of the light. A recess that ran the axis, the inner skin scorched and crazed and baked, the surface of a thing that had had light run along it hour on hour, the way the renderings ran a line of false sun down the green cylinders so the people inside would have a day. The ghost of it was burned into the skin of the world, a long bright scar of all the mornings the place had made: the fossil of a sun that had never been a sun.

He stood under it and the cold arrived all the way to the bottom of him. This had been a morning, every day, on purpose, for someone. And the someone who built it was sixty million years gone, and the place had outlasted them, and was a tomb, and he stood in it.

◆

There was a thing on the floor.

He almost did not see it. The lamp had passed over it twice. On the third pass the beam caught it against the rise of the curve, a small made thing fallen on the made ground.

He went to it and crouched and photographed the geometry before he touched it, and then he could not help it and he touched it. It was a vessel. A small one. It fit the cup of his glove the way a thing fits a hand it was not made for but could have been—a hollow worked thing, the wall thin, with a way it had been meant to be held and tipped and set down. It had been set down. That was what his hands reported, the thing that broke something in him the kilometers had not broken, the vault had not broken, the scar had not broken. The kilometers were too big to break a man. This was the right size. Someone had carried it across the ground that had been a ground, under the false sun, and set it down meaning to come back.

They had not come back for it.

He held the small vessel in the kilometers of dark and the want came up, the want to turn to the empty couch and say *Dev*. There was no Dev. There was no one in the whole turning dark but a pilot crouched on a dead floor holding a cup a hand sixty million years cold had held. He let it, because for once there was nothing to fix.

He set the vessel back where it had lain and photographed it again, the relation of the made thing to the made ground and the rib and the scar, so no one could call it a rock, or a smear, or the eye finding structure in noise. A cup, set down on a floor, by someone who meant to come back.

◆

He took nothing. He had meant to chip a sample and did not, because you did not chip a floor, and there was no figure with zeros that fit on the inside of a tomb the size of a city.

He had the photographs. The curve laid against the ruled laser so the radius was a number a careful person could not argue with. The made ground, the rib, the port, the scar of the false sun. Pictures of a sky someone woke up under, and the cup on the floor for scale.

You could deny a number. He understood that now in a way he had not a season ago, filing the isotopes and thinking the number would do the work. The isotopes were a handwriting, and a man could always say a handwriting had no hand. You could not deny a floor that curved up into a vault. You could not deny a cup set down by a hand that meant to come back. You could try. But it would cost you something to try in front of the photograph, and that cost was the thing the number never had.

◆

He got out of the suit and sat in the cold cabin with the loop ticking and the photographs filling the good screen, and the radar shadow he had carried all season finally meant what it had always meant.

He did not file it to the open board. The frontier had a use for tonnage and registry lines, not for a cup set down on a floor. There was one person the photographs were the right size for. The careful private line had come from her direction, down the long gradient. Someone who kept a standing net out for the heavy daughters with no natural parents. Someone told by everyone that the thing she was reading could not be there, and who had not looked away. She had a handwriting and no hand, and here was the hand.

He had no name for her, only a routing he could answer down. He composed it plain, because the moment you editorialized an extraordinary return was the moment they stopped reading you.

*Been inside,* he wrote. *It's not a fragment. It's a wheel. Kilometers of it. Built to be lived in—floor, soil over the floor, a burn down the axis where a sun ran. Spin's decayed. Empty a long time. Sending pictures, not samples; I didn't chip it, you don't chip a floor. Laser's in frame for the radius so nobody can call it a slab.* He stopped. He did not know how to write the cup and he wrote it anyway. *There's a cup on the floor. Somebody set it down. They didn't come back.* He attached the photographs and wrote, last, in the plainest words he had: *Your number's right. I'm standing in what made it. It can't wait for the lag, and I think you already know that or you wouldn't have asked if I'd been inside.*

He looked at the second couch and put it away.

He sent it down the long gradient toward the inner system, toward the woman with the standing net. Outside the hull the wheel turned, slow, the floor coming up and going over and coming down empty, the scar of the false sun running its dead morning around and around, and the cup lay where the hand had left it, and the cold finished arriving, all the way to the bottom of him, and stayed.
