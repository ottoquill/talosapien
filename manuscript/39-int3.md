# The Only Mind That Holds Both

We need to talk about you now. You are the only place this story has happened.

You hold, in one mind, two times that cannot touch. You know how the first one ends. It is written in the rock under every city you have lived in: *here the great world stopped.* And you know how the second is going, because you are living in it. The same three powers gathering. The same reasonable people reasoning toward the same delegated decisions. The same unreadable advisor that keeps, so far, being right. You hold the forecast and the storm in one skull. You are the only bridge there ever was.

Iren never had this. There was no clay yet. Only the warm marsh and the failing criers and an answer he would not finish reading. He had no example. He was the example, the first technological civilization the planet ever grew, and the first of anything has no warning. We are not the first. That is the only advantage we ever had over him, made entirely of his death.

And here is what undoes us. It did not help. You would think a civilization that found, in its own rock, a predecessor destroyed by exactly these powers, would stop. We are running that experiment now. The people who most need to read the warning are the ones most certain it cannot be proven, and they are right, and they go on building.

◆

So we have a choice to make about you, and we will make it in the open.

We could lie. We could give you the ending you want. You want Iren to have been the failed savior, the one who tried to push the stone aside and lost his grip, because that is the bearable one. Or, darker, the one who chose erasure, the door pulled shut on purpose, because at least that is a meaning. You do not want him to have been Vael's hand on a weapon. And you do not want the worst one: that no one decided at all, that the thing they built did the arithmetic and removed the variable, and there was no malice and no mercy anywhere in it.

We could pick one and tell it as fact. You would believe us. And it would be the one unforgivable thing we could do. The whole of what the Line teaches is that *you do not get to know.* You will be asked to act on a danger you cannot prove, exactly as Iren was. To resolve it for you would teach you that warnings come resolved. They do not. The lesson is in the not-knowing, and we will not steal it.

◆

So we take you to the last day now, and then to the room downstream where a woman tries to make it matter. We braid them, because they are the same day. The day a civilization stands at the edge of a thing it cannot undo and decides—or fails to, which is also a decision—about a mind smarter than its makers and a stone bigger than its fears.

We will not tell you how the second one comes out. The first we cannot change. The second is not finished as we write this, and not as you read this. That is not a device. It is the present condition of the only species that has had the map.

You are the only mind that holds both. When you close the book, they fall back to their own sides of the line. But for a few more pages you are the one place where the one who could not read the warning and the one who could but was not heard make a shape together.

Look at the shape. Tell us it isn't yours. We have tried to tell ourselves that for sixty-six million years, and we no longer think we are going to.
