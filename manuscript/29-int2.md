# The Recoverability Budget

Let us tell you what sixty-six million years does.

It subducts. The floor of the ocean they sailed has slid back into the mantle and ceased to exist. Not eroded. Unmade. Returned to the melt, and every record on it with it. It erodes. The mountains they may have named are ground to sand. It buries, and the burial cooks, smearing the delicate thing into the ambient until signal and noise are the same substance.

Against all of that, here is the budget—the whole of what a civilization can leave to a reader a hundred thousand lifetimes downstream.

It can leave a chemical anomaly, if it is global and sharp and made of something the planet does not make. The iridium qualifies. The transuranics qualify, barely, because their daughters decay on a clock we can read. The shifted isotopes qualify only as a whisper, deniable, a thing you can hear or not depending on what you came hoping to hear.

It can leave a physical object, if it was large and far from the grinding surface. Out in the cold, in the belt of stones, where a million years is an afternoon. It is the only thing in this account you could put your hand on, and a man is about to. It will change nothing for the people who have decided in advance. A thing too strange to explain is, for a certain mind, simply a thing not yet explained—and that mind is not wrong, only fatally late.

And it can leave bone, marked. A few teeth. The negative impression of a worked edge. The deathbed of a single afternoon, if a flood sealed the dying from the air fast enough. We have that afternoon, in unbearable detail, the fish with the falling sky in their gills. It has told us everything except the one thing we would trade all the rest to know.

◆

It cannot leave the why.

Intent does not fossilize. A decision leaves the same trace whether made in malice, in terror, in error, or in a love so large it could not tell itself from despair. The clay records that a thing happened. It cannot record what it was *for*.

So when the object falls out of the dark toward the shallow sea, we will tell you the mass and the speed and the angle and the hour and the height of the wave. We will not tell you whether it was a weapon, loosed to end a war by ending a world; or a shield that failed; or a door, closed on purpose, by someone who decided the only safe number of such civilizations was none, including their own.

All three are consistent with the clay. The murderer and the suicide and the failed savior leave the same crater. You will want, badly, to be told which one Iren was. We want it more. We have loved him longer. We do not know, and the not-knowing is not a gap in our work. It is the finished shape of the truth.

> *Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair: / Nothing beside remains.*

The traveler reads a face in the sand and a boast on a pedestal. He has more than we have. We have the iridium and the strange glass and the marked bone and, in the cold, the turning thing that stopped turning. No face. No words. Only the level sand, and the certainty that someone built high enough to fall this far.

We go on now, into the turn, into the war, into the object. Everything we show you with such confidence is a kindness laid over a blank. We earned the confidence honestly. The blank is honest too. Hold them in separate hands.
