# Tombstone or Letter

Go back now to the line in the rock. We have been away a whole book and it has not moved. It is the one thing that waits.

We have shown you everything we have. The man at the clutch and the woman at the core. The turning worlds in the cold and the day the sky came down. And the room, downstream, where a tired scientist tried to convert a centimeter of clay into a change of course, and we cut away before the vote. We did it on purpose.

So we will not give you a fact. We will give you the line, the two things it can be, and the choice.

◆

If it is a tombstone, the story is closed and clean, the oldest story. A civilization rose on a young warm planet. It built fire it could not bank and minds it could not read and powers no one is meant to hold at once. It held them anyway, because it was first and had no warning. It fell. The planet folded the evidence into a centimeter of clay, and a second civilization rose in the cleared ground and read the one sentence the dead can still say: *we were here, and we did this, and we could not stop ourselves, and you are standing on the proof.* Then we are the mourners.

But.

If it is a letter, it is not closed, and it was never only about them. A letter is written to be read by someone not yet dead. And this one is addressed in our own hand. The fixed nitrogen, the split atom, the turning habitats, the delegated mind—a list of things we are, this year, proud to be building. The message is not *we were here.* It is *we were exactly where you are, and felt exactly as safe, and here is what it cost.* A tombstone records a death. A letter requests a reply.

We cannot tell you which it is. The clay does not say. A tombstone and a letter are, in the rock, the identical object. The only thing that makes one a letter is a living hand that opens it.

The difference is not in the clay.

It is in the reader.

◆

The people who study the silence of the sky wonder why no one has ever called. They speak of a filter, some step on the road from chemistry to a voice that almost nothing gets past, and argue whether it is behind or ahead. On this one small planet, that road was walked twice. The first walker did not make it. We found the evidence by accident, forty years late, and have been told ever since that we are probably mistaken. If the filter is a thing that happens once a civilization can reach for the powers that end it—look at the Line, and tell us it is not—then it is not ahead of us and not behind us. It is level with us. It is the step we are taking right now.

They thought they would be the ones to hold it. That one thing we did not have to invent. You do not build the Ark, or split the atom, or make the mind that reads the weather, unless you believe in the marrow you will be the exception. The marsh was full of that belief. So is the room downstream. So is yours.

◆

We will leave you the way we found him, at the count.

Somewhere below the line a man we named for the metal of his own destruction lays one finger along the curve of four shells, in a light whose true color we cannot see, counting a future he cannot save and will not abandon. He does not know the count is already a goodbye. And somewhere above the line a woman does the same, running an impossible number once more, keeping a record by hand because it is the only fidelity she can offer the dead and the unborn. She does not know how it comes out. Neither do we. Neither do you.

That is where it ends, because that is where it is. Not in the answer. There is no answer. It is in the centimeter between two pale beds of stone, which is either the last word of a closed life or the first line of an open letter, and which has never once been up to us.

It was always going to be up to whoever read it in time.

You have read it.

There is still time.

We do not know how much.

Neither did they.
